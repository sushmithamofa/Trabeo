1. What this document is for

We generate the structural Revit model from your CAD drawing instead of redrawing it by hand. A script reads the DWG layers, matches each shape to its mark, joins the mark to the schedule row, and places the Revit element. Anything the script cannot read with certainty is not guessed; it comes back to you as a written query.

This only works if the drawing is consistent in a few specific ways. This document sets out those ways.

What we are not asking for. We are not asking you to model in Revit, to change how the drawing looks when printed, to redraw existing work, or to adopt a new CAD package. Every rule below is about how entities are placed on layers in the DWG you already produce.

What changes for you. Roughly six drafting habits, listed in section 5, plus three schedules delivered as Excel alongside the DWG. Nothing in section 5 takes longer to draw than what is being drawn today.

Why it is worth it. On the audited file, 4,200 entities carry the whole first floor of one wing. Reading them automatically takes minutes. Redrawing them takes days, and takes days again at every reissue.

4. Drawing setup

4.1 Units and scale

Millimetres, drawn 1:1. INSUNITS = 4, MEASUREMENT = 1. Nothing scaled, stretched or exploded. The current file already complies.

4.2 One floor per file

Model space holds the structural plan for one floor only. Schedules, legends, details, title block and key plan may sit beside the plan in model space, but on their own layers (section 6) so they can be ignored by the reader.

4.3 Flat at zero

Every entity at Z = 0. Run FLATTEN before issue, then check that EXTMIN and EXTMAX both report Z = 0.

In the audited file: 375 entities carry a Z other than zero — 244 of them at Z = −229, and others at −11,425, −11,197, −8,962, +7,506, +14,784. Drawing extents report a Z range of −22,493 to 30,544,084 mm. A script reading plan coordinates has to discard Z entirely, which also means it cannot trust any Z it does find.

4.4 Origin and setting out

  • One base point, shared with the architectural model, agreed once for the whole project.
  • The same XY position for every floor and every wing. A floor must sit directly above the floor below with no shift.
  • Never re-rotate the plan between issues. The current 2 degree rotation is fine and can stay; it simply must not change.
  • Mark the shared base point with a single point or small cross on layer S-REF.

In the audited file: the plan occupies about 25.4 m x 56.3 m, positioned roughly 2.9 km from the drawing origin, with nothing identifying a shared point.

4.5 Grid

The structural grid goes on S-GRID: grid lines as lines, bubbles as circles with the grid label as text inside. If the structural grid is the architect's grid, copy it in from the architectural file rather than redrawing it.

In the audited file: there is no grid of any kind. Columns are numbered 1 to 32 with no lines to locate them against. There are 34 dimensions on the DIM layer, but with no grid there is nothing for them to dimension from.

4.6 Housekeeping before issue

  • OVERKILL run, so no entity is drawn twice on top of another.
  • PURGE run, so empty layers, unused blocks and unused styles are gone.
  • AUDIT run, with errors fixed.
  • No structural element inside a block. Blocks are fine for title blocks, logos, section marks and stair symbols; not for columns, beams, slabs or walls.
  • Layer names spelled one way only.

In the audited file: 10 of 51 layers carry no entities; RCC PARDI and R.C.C. PARDI both exist as layers with only the second one used; 2 column outlines are exact duplicates lying on top of each other. 69 block definitions exist, none of which contain structural elements, which is correct.

5. How each element is drawn

5.1 Columns

  • One closed rectangle per column, drawn at the scheduled size, on S-COL.
  • Columns that stop at this floor go on S-COL-STOP instead, so we know not to carry them up.
  • Floating columns go on S-COL-FLOAT.
  • Nothing else on these layers. No hatch, no text, no detail linework.

In the audited file: 68 outlines on COLUMN, of which 61 are closed rectangles that de-rotate to exact schedule sizes, 2 are closed 17-point outlines (the lift walls), and 5 are open 5-point polylines that a script cannot fill. A further 8 rectangles of 495 x 1141 sit on the COLUMN layer at 0 degrees, a size that appears nowhere in the column schedule; please confirm what these are. The 6 outlines on FL COL are all correctly closed.

5.2 Walls

Lift walls (LW-1 to LW-4) and the RCC pardi walls go on S-WALL as closed outlines, with their thickness as drawn. They are not columns and must not sit on the column layer.

In the audited file: the lift wall pieces are drawn on COLUMN as 230 x 2110 and 230 x 1950 rectangles and as two 17-point outlines. The RCC pardi is on R.C.C. PARDI, one closed and one open outline.

5.3 Beams and girders — the most important rule

  • One centreline per span, on S-BEAM for beams and S-GIRDER for girders.
  • If drawn as a multiline, set justification to Zero, not Top or Bottom.
  • One entity type throughout. Pick multiline or polyline and stay with it.
  • Girders drawn at the scheduled width, not at a single nominal width for all of them.
  • Every line parallel to the grid, or at the true angle of that part of the building.

In the audited file:

Top justification means the line in the file lies on one face of the beam, 115 mm from its centre (175 mm for a 350 girder). A script can shift a line by 115 mm, but nothing in the file says which side to shift it to, and guessing wrong puts a beam a full width away from its columns.

Beam multilines are clean on angle: 59 at 92 degrees and 56 at 2 degrees. The plain lines are not: 13 run at 69 degrees, which is the angled wing and correct, but 2 run at 0 degrees. On GIRDER BEAM, 2 lines run at 0 degrees, 2 at 85 and 2 at 6, where 2 or 92 is expected.

5.4 Slabs

  • One closed outline per slab panel, on S-SLAB.
  • Edges land on the beam centrelines, or on the slab edge where there is no beam.
  • One tag per panel, even where several panels share the same slab type.

In the audited file: the SLAB layer holds 6 open polylines of 3 and 4 points. There is nothing closed to convert into a Revit floor. The 65 slab tags cover 19 unique marks, so several panels share a mark: S1 is used 14 times, S3 12 times, S4 8 times, S8 6 times. Each of those placements needs its own outline.

5.5 Sunk areas, openings and stairs

  • A closed outline for each sunk zone on S-SUNK-100, S-SUNK-150 or S-SUNK-400, named for its depth in millimetres. Add layers for other depths as the same pattern.
  • Pool and deck slabs on S-SUNK-POOL and S-SUNK-DECK.
  • A hatch may sit on top for printing, but the closed outline must exist. A hatch alone is not enough.
  • Openings and cut-outs as closed outlines on S-OPEN.
  • Stairs and landings as closed outlines on S-STAIR.

In the audited file: sunk zones exist only as hatches, 12 on 300mm SUNK (pattern ANSI33) and 7 on 900MM SUNK (pattern FLEX), with 1 more on SUNK and 10 on HATCH. The legend on the same sheet lists 100MM SUNK, 150MM SUNK, 400MM SUNK, POOL SLAB and DECK SLAB. The layer names and the legend do not agree, and we cannot tell which hatch is which depth.

5.6 What must not be mixed

One layer, one kind of thing. Do not place tags, hatches, dimensions or detail linework on an element layer, and do not place two element types on one layer.

7.2 Placement

  1. One mark per text object. A text object reading B107/BP or /B60 describes two elements and cannot be split reliably. Use two text objects.
  2. Within 300 mm of the element it labels, or inside it. Where a leader is needed for clarity in print, keep the text within 300 mm and let the leader run the other way.
  3. One tag per element, once. No duplicated tags, no tag without an element, no element without a tag.
  4. Real text or mtext, never exploded to lines.

In the audited file: tag placement is the single most uneven area.

8. Schedules as data

Send the column, beam and slab schedules as Excel, in addition to drawing them on the sheet. The drawn schedule stays exactly as it is for printing; the Excel is a parallel delivery, not a replacement.

Rules

  1. One row per mark. Grouped rows are split before delivery: B-2,4,7 | 230x600 becomes three rows.
  2. One value per cell. No units inside the cell, no ranges, no "as above", no merged cells.
  3. A header row with stable column names that do not change between issues.
  4. Every row complete. A blank depth or an unreadable size stops that element being modelled.

Use the workbook issued with this document. J1153_C_01_STR_2026-07-14.xlsx carries one sheet per element type with the columns already set out, the marks we read from your drawing already entered in the Mark column, and dropdowns on the fields that must match a fixed list. Its Read Me sheet repeats these rules.
