**Claude** · 2026-10-22 08:51

Coordinates are the main problem with this feature. I'd solve them in layers: the app tries automatic methods first and asks you only when they fail. In every case the result is shown to you to confirm, which keeps to our "nothing assumed" rule.

## Why the coordinates don't match
- **Structure:** the app builds the structural model in the DWG's own model-space coordinates, in mm. Each drawing set has one origin, and the floors are stacked by lining up their columns.
- **Architecture:** a Revit or IFC model has its own origin: Internal Origin, Project Base Point or Survey Point. On export the architect chooses which one, and they may also apply rotation (True North vs Project North).

The two models can therefore differ in five ways:
1. **Units:** mm, m, feet.
2. **Shift:** X/Y offset.
3. **Rotation:** about the vertical axis.
4. **Level:** finished floor vs structural slab, or ±0.000 at a different place.
5. Rarely, **mirroring**. This would be an error, and the app should flag it rather than "fix" it.

## How the app would solve it, in order

**Step 1: Check if they already match**
- In practice this is common. Consultants usually draw on the architect's CAD background, and architects often link CAD "origin to origin" in Revit. Our own drawings even contain the architect's background beyond the plan.
- So the app first converts units and checks: do the structural columns land inside the architect's columns and walls? If most do, it's aligned and nothing else is needed.

**Step 2: Use location data in the file**
- IFC files can carry their placement (site location, map conversion, true north). If present, the app uses it and checks again the same way.

**Step 3: Automatic match by columns (the main method)**
- Architectural models almost always contain the columns, as architectural columns or a linked structural model.
- The app takes the column centres from both models and searches for the one shift + rotation that puts the most of them on top of each other, within about 25–50 mm. This is the standard technique for matching point patterns. It's reliable because a column grid is a distinctive pattern.
- The same can work with wall lines or the building outline if there are no columns.
- Result shown to you: "Aligned: 22 of 24 columns match within 20 mm. Shift X +125.40 m, Y −38.20 m, rotation 0°."

**Step 4: Manual fallback (two points)**
- If automatic fails, you click two points in the structure (for example grid A/1 and A/8 crossings, or two corner columns) and the same two in the architecture. Two points fix shift and rotation exactly.
- There'd be a nudge control (move by X/Y mm, rotate by degrees) for fine-tuning.

**Step 5: Levels (vertical)**
- IFC storeys have names and elevations ("First Floor +4.200"). The app matches them against its own level table by name and height.
- It shows the difference per floor ("architect FFL is 50 mm above structural top of slab on every floor"). You accept it as one offset, or per floor if they vary.

**Step 6: Save and reuse**
- The alignment is saved with the project, per architectural file.
- When the architect sends a revised model, the app reuses the same alignment and checks it still fits. If the columns no longer match, it warns you rather than silently drawing it wrong.

## Side benefit: the check itself is a review
The column match tells us where architecture and structure disagree, for example: "4 columns don't match: C12 is 300 mm off the architect's column at grid D/5." Each of these can become a ready-made comment for the consultant. Often that's exactly the coordination error you want to catch.

## One thing to ask the architect
To make step 1 or 2 work most of the time, a standard request helps: *"Export IFC with Internal Origin (or Shared Coordinates), and keep the CAD linked origin-to-origin."* The app works without this, but with it the alignment is automatic.

## Limitations to be aware of
- **No grid names yet.** The app reads grid lines but doesn't yet read grid names (A, B, 1, 2), which is still an open item. Matching by columns doesn't need them. But for manual picking, "pick grid A/1" would be nicer than "pick a point", so a grid-name reader would be a useful add-on here too.
- **Different geometry per floor.** If the architect's model is a different design stage from the structural drawings, fewer columns will match. The app would show the match percentage so you can judge.

Do your architects' Revit models usually have the structural columns in them? That decides whether the column match works out of the box for your projects.
