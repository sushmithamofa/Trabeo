## What blocks a plain "put it on a website"

Online options are Autodesk's paid cloud Revit service (APS Design Automation, charged per use) or a small add-in installed on each user's PC. 3. **A website can't freely write to someone's drive.** To keep data on their drive, either:
   - they download the results (the app already makes the zip package), and the server deletes its copy afterwards; or
   - in Chrome/Edge, the page asks them once to pick a folder on their PC and then saves the project there.

   In either case the drawings pass through the server while they are analysed. They don't have to stay there.

| | **A. Online server** | **B. Installed on each PC** | **C. Online + small Revit add-in** |

## How Snaptrude and similar apps store data

Snaptrude runs in the browser and keeps projects on its own cloud servers, not on the user's drive. That is the normal pattern for online apps (Snaptrude, Autodesk's cloud tools, Figma, Speckle):

- **Files** (uploaded drawings, models) go to cloud storage such as Amazon S3 or Azure, in a separate area per account.
- **Project details** (names, settings, who can see what) go in a database.
- **The user's PC** keeps only a temporary browser cache. The user downloads or exports when they want a file.
- **Safety:** isolation between accounts, encryption, automatic backups, and a promise in the terms about where data is kept and when it is deleted. Enterprise customers can often choose the region, or delete their data.

So saving each user's data on their own drive is actually unusual for online tools. Online apps keep it on their servers because:
- it works from any PC;
- a team can share one project;
- nothing is lost if a laptop dies;
- updates never have to touch the user's machine.

The desktop tools (Revit, ETABS) are the ones that store on the local drive.

## What this means for your commercial launch

The simplest and most standard route is to do what you have now, but on a rented cloud server instead of your PC:
- each customer's projects in their own area on that server;
- downloads exactly as now;
- a clear rule such as "your drawings are deleted 30 days after the project closes, or whenever you press Delete".

Customers see no code, you don't need your PC on, and it is how they already expect online tools to work.
