**Claude** · 2026-10-07 07:46

No, it isn't built only for those drawings. But you've put your finger on the real risk, so here is the honest picture.

**What's general**
- No reader looks for these files, layer names or coordinates. Each one looks for how structural drawings are drawn in general:
  - stair treads are evenly spaced equal lines;
  - a schedule is a grid with a mark row and level bands;
  - a section's size comes from its dimensions, at whatever scale the drawing uses;
  - an L-shaped "wall" the size of a column is a column.
- Layer names are never relied on. For each new consultant the tool suggests a layer profile from what is drawn on each layer, and that profile is kept per consultant.
- When it can't decide, it reports instead of guessing, so a new drawing style should show up as "missing" or "check this", not as wrong numbers.

**What's still a risk**
- The samples are 3 consultants' drawings (J1153, Sasan Gir, Villa 5). Each time I met a new style here, I generalised the rule and added a test. But real drawings from other offices will certainly have conventions none of these 3 use, for example schedules laid out differently or levels written another way.
- Some limits are set from these drawings, such as the 2.5 m label reach and the 1200 mm cut-off for a column-sized "wall". They are reasonable values, but only proven on these three.
- Stage 7 (building sections and elevations) has almost no real test data. None of the samples has a full building section, so that reader is tested only on drawings I constructed myself.

## How it handles different consultants today

- **Where sources disagree or nothing gives a value**, it's shown in Review. Nothing is guessed.

So the only non-dynamic part is the MOFA hatch table, which is fixed in the settings.

## A completely different hatch
A hatch pattern alone has no meaning: nothing in a cross-hatch says "250 mm". The meaning has to be written somewhere in the drawings. A fully dynamic app collects every place it could be:

1. **A legend anywhere in the set**, not just on the same sheet. Your MOFA legend would then be imported as a normal drawing and read like any consultant's, not kept as a fixed table.
2. **Text inside or next to the hatched area**, e.g. "SUNK 150", "−0.150", "FFL −250".
3. **The slab schedule**: a mark like S4A listed with its sunk depth.
4. **Section drawings**, where the step is measured from the dimension.
5. **Level marks** on the plan.
6. **Matching hatches by their look**: pattern, angle, spacing and colour. This also covers exploded hatches that are just lines.

Each of these becomes evidence:
- sources agree: the depth is used;
- sources disagree: it's shown in Review;
- nothing states it: it's listed as "depth unknown" for you to fill in once.
